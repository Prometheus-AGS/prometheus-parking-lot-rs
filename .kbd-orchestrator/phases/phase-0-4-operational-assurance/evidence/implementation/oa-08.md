## Delta and named failure

Issue #81 identifies accepted ResourcePool work lost when a legal `Spawn`
implementation discards its future without polling it. Previously,
`launch` passed the async `supervise` future to `Spawn`; its settlement guard
was created only in the unpolled body. Dropping that future returned its RAII
permit but produced no terminal/mailbox outcome and left the ledger accepted.
Shutdown consequently waited for work that could never settle.

Task1 makes `supervise` a synchronous future factory. Its `UnpolledRun`
guard owns the scheduled task and permit before the handoff to `Spawn`.
Task2 documents the resulting behavior; it adds no production behavior.

## Observable behavior and report interval

With returning, non-panicking clock/queue collaborators and a functioning
mailbox, dropping an unpolled accepted run:

1. Emits one `LifecycleEvent::Failed`, with no `Started` or executor call.
2. Completes with `TaskStatus::Failed(TaskError::Cancelled { reason })`, where
   the reason is exactly `spawner discarded resource pool task before its first poll`.
   A configured mailbox key receives one delivery attempt and no result payload.
3. Returns its permit through `Dispatcher::complete` and launches newly
   admitted parked work before mailbox/audit callbacks. Capacity is available
   before a mailbox consumer can observe the result.
4. Increments `failed_tasks` and the ledger's finished-outcome tally, then
   settles the accepted count as the discard action exits.

Although the error variant is `TaskError::Cancelled`, this path is failure
settlement. It emits `Failed`, does not emit the shutdown `Cancelled` event,
does not call the asynchronous cancellation sink and does not capture/requeue
the payload. It does not increment `ShutdownReport::cancelled` or its sink
sub-tallies. A discard during an active shutdown counts in `report.completed`
(that field includes finished failures); one settled before shutdown began
is outside the report's interval. Both leave `unsettled == 0` for those runs.

The `Spawn` trait has no acceptance result or polling promise. A spawner may
drop immediately or retain the future and drop it later. Settlement is
synchronous on that dropping thread and needs no replacement runtime. A
spawner that retains a future forever without polling or dropping it still
retains its task/permit and can hold shutdown until its deadline. Runtime
availability and eventual polling are owned by the spawner. This fix does
not create a runtime, restart execution, or add crash durability.

## Ownership, drop order and synchronization reasoning

Static reasoning, not an executed proof:

- Before `Spawn::spawn`, the guard owns `Some((task, permit))` and a discard
  closure holding an `Arc<Inner>`. Dropping the future before first poll runs
  that closure once. No clone of the task payload is needed.
- On first poll, the async body captures the whole guard, establishes the
  existing `SettleOnDrop`, then takes the pair. There is no await between
  establishing settlement and taking ownership. The emptied unpolled guard
  cannot invoke the discard action. The existing `reported` flag and panic
  backstop continue to govern the running path.
- On discard, `SettleOnDrop` is outside `catch_unwind`: callbacks or payload
  drop unwinding cannot skip ledger settlement when unwinding is caught.
  `finish` releases capacity and launches admitted work before mailbox/audit
  callbacks. The task payload is then dropped; it is never executed on this
  path. A caught panic payload is forgotten rather than risking another
  panic from its destructor, following the incumbent containment approach.
- Launch invokes the spawner after the dispatcher has released its mutex.
  The new guard adds no lock or await. The holding-spawner fixture removes
  its futures from its own mutex before dropping them, since completion may
  reenter that same spawner to launch parked work.
- No atomic operation, ordering or UnsafeCell path was added or changed.
  Dispatcher completion retains its mutex unlock-to-subsequent-lock edge
  covering release and admission. The ledger's finished tally is sequenced
  before its `SeqCst` in-flight decrement (a release RMW); the shutdown
  `SeqCst` load observing settlement acquires the preceding effects.
  Existing `watching`/counter `SeqCst` operations and `Notify` registration
  retain the documented counter-change/waiter handshake. The task-local
  `reported` flag retains `Relaxed` accesses, ordered by that task's polls;
  it does not publish state to another task. These are unchanged edges,
  not new ordering selections or an executed model-test result.

## Limits, migration and verification disposition

No caller signature or data-format migration is introduced. Callers that
previously received no result for discarded work can now observe the failure
above and distinguish it by the cancellation reason. Keep the runtime alive
for work intended to execute; a custom spawner must eventually poll or drop
each supplied future to provide progress. Any application retry remains an
application decision for this in-memory pool.

The uncomfortable limit is that a configured mailbox can still fail to store
the failure: `deliver` errors are logged without retry, and a panic before
storage cannot establish a mailbox result. The fixture covers a panic after
successful storage only. The discard catcher contains unwinding; it does not
make a blocking callback return, survive process abort, or ensure remaining
completion steps run after a panicking clock/queue interrupts them. Hook
delivery retains the existing bounded queue and dropped-event accounting.
No blanket guarantee of result delivery under arbitrary callback failure is
claimed. The existing #57 `prune_expired` terminal-event/mailbox gap is
preserved here; its resolution belongs to oa-10.
